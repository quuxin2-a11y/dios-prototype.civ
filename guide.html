One world

Space to street, in one smooth map.

Every layer, setting and selection is shared. Turn on aircraft in one world and they're there in the others.

The map

The Dark Globe

The whole planet as a sleek dark map: glowing country borders, state lines, and names that appear as you zoom, from countries down to street names. Every camera, aircraft, ship, data layer and undersea cable is drawn on the phone's graphics chip, so it stays smooth with over 100,000 points. Tap anything to open it.

Zoom into any city and it turns 3D by itself: aerial photos fade in, buildings rise and the view tilts. Zoom out and it levels again.

Above it

The Sky

The night sky over wherever you're looking, right now: Orion and the other constellations, about 5,000 stars, the Moon in its real phase, and the planets. A second view shows the solar system on today's orbits.

Get started

Up and running in a minute.

No account, no sign-up, no cost.

InstallDownload the latest Android app from the project's GitHub page and open it. Android will ask you to allow apps from your browser; that's normal for apps outside the Play Store. iPhone and computers can use the web version.
Look aroundThe app opens on the dark globe with your cameras and layers already there. Drag to move, pinch to zoom from space to the street, and tap a point to watch a camera or see what it is.
Turn on layersOpen Layers and switch on anything from flights and storms to undersea cables. They're grouped by pack so they're easy to find.
Go deeperSearch any place, zoom into a city to see it in 3D, tap a point to open it, or tap the logo for the sky.

Trust

Open data, handled responsibly.

Built to show the world, not to watch people.

◎Public sources onlyCameras and data their owners publish on purpose: transport departments, weather and science agencies, open maps and public registries. Unsecured private cameras are excluded.
⌂Your data stays yoursNo accounts, no ads, no tracking. Your location is used only while a feature needs it. Settings, saved places and keys never leave your phone.
⚙Built in the openDeveloped in public on GitHub, signed with the developer's key, and checked automatically on every build with tests and security scanning.
What it will never do. Read licence plates, identify people or vehicles, track individuals, show phone locations, or list private individuals' names, arrest records, sex-offender registries or voter files. Records show organisations only.

Questions

Good to know.

Is it free?

Yes. Everything runs on free public sources. A few optional extras use free keys of your own, added in Sources & keys.

Is it safe to install?

Yes. It's built in the open on GitHub from public code, signed with the developer's own key, and tested and security-scanned on every build. Android shows a warning for any app from outside the Play Store; that's about where it came from, not what's in it.

Is the 3D view AI-generated?

No. Close in, the dark globe shows real aerial photos (Esri World Imagery) with buildings raised to their mapped heights from OpenStreetMap. It's real data, so it's accurate, and it's one smooth map rather than a separate 3D world.

How accurate is the sky?

Star positions come from a published star catalogue, and the Moon and planets are calculated on your phone from published orbital formulas. It's accurate enough to find Orion, the Moon and the bright planets over your city tonight.

Why do some layers show nothing?

Many layers cover specific places (for example traffic incidents in Austin and Calgary, or US border crossings) or only appear when you're zoomed in to city level. Each layer's status line says when that's the case.

Do I have to wait for everything to load?

No. Every 20 minutes the project's own data service gathers all the public camera networks and worldwide layers into one compact download, so the app gets everything in a second or two instead of asking each source itself. The phone also keeps the cameras and data layers from your last session, so they appear the moment the app opens and are refreshed in the background. A layer that's being refreshed shows UPDATING… next to it.

Why does the camera count change?

Each network's fresh list replaces the saved one as it arrives, and the worldwide webcam list keeps building for a few minutes. Networks that don't answer are retried automatically, and their cameras from last time stay on the map in the meantime.

How do I update?

On Android, download the newest file from the same GitHub link and install it over the old one. Your settings and keys stay. The web version updates itself.

Can the app go back to the old screen?

No. The dark globe and its dashboard are the app's only screen, and no setting or older saved choice can bring back the old one. If the dark globe ever can't load, a simpler globe with the same dashboard and all your data stands in, and the dark globe is retried automatically until it's back.

The map flickers or shows lines on my phone

Some phone graphics chips, such as the Pixel 10's, draw the map engine's round globe with flicker and lines. Open Settings → Display and choose Smooth globe: from space you get a round globe the app draws itself, and zooming in to a region turns it into the flat map, which those chips draw cleanly; zoom back out and the round globe returns. Phones with that chip are set to Smooth globe automatically. Flat map keeps the flat map at every zoom. Your cameras, layers and data stay exactly the same, and you can switch back any time.

What if the screen goes black?

The app now repairs this itself. If the phone takes the globe's graphics away, the globe redraws within a couple of seconds. If the dark globe can't draw, a simpler globe with the same dashboard and all your data stands in, and the dark globe is retried automatically. If it still happens, send the error report from Briefing → Settings → System check and error report.

Something isn't working

In Layers, open Live networks and tap Copy short status. It shows which sources are answering, without your keys, and you can share it with whoever is helping you.
